# Other animals

The book's House has sea creatures and vegetation in the Lower Halls (fish, mussels, crabs, lobsters, an octopus, a great many carved ones) and birds in the Middle (design/birds.md). It has no living mice
or rats: its mice are carved, in a statue of a child and mice that eat a sack of grain. The owner's reading is that animals now and then stray in from outside through the portal, and that the House's
life is that of the author's own England; so, **by the owner's decision, an occasional mouse or rat**: a stray, rare, never a population.

Plan (not built yet; the birds come first):
* **Mouse:** skitter and a small scratch, a gnawing, a thin squeak, in the middle halls and the derelict ones, at night more than by day, and very rarely. A mouse is mostly heard, almost never seen.
* **Rat:** a heavier scamper and a gnaw, a scrabble on stone, a squeal; near the fish and the gulls' nests (the lower stairs and the gull hall), rarer than the mouse.
* Both are **silent when you are still and close**; they flee. Both are in the narration and the scene as a stray visitor (the rule of one world model: what the sound says, the text says).
